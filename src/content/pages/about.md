I’m **Mofiyinfoluwa Orekoya**, a Computer Science undergraduate at Purdue University. I’m interested in systems, ML infrastructure, computer architecture, and the software that sits between algorithms and hardware.

I like building infrastructure that other software can build upon: runtimes, schedulers, developer tooling, data pipelines, and systems that make better use of heterogeneous compute.

## The kinds of problems I come back to

One idea has increasingly shaped what I enjoy working on: **I like building software that makes other people more capable.**

Compilers, runtimes, languages, and developer tools interest me because their value compounds. A better application helps its users; a better platform can help people build entirely new applications.

I’m particularly curious about how workloads should be scheduled across increasingly diverse hardware, and what abstractions we need to make that compute useful. But I don’t want to restrict myself to one corner of computer science. If something presents a difficult systems problem or an opportunity to build useful infrastructure, I’m probably interested.

## At Purdue, and before this semester

I’m pursuing Computer Science with a minor in **Microelectronics & Semiconductors**, focused on the microelectronics track. I expect to graduate in 2028 and am considering graduate study in computer engineering or computer science, particularly systems, architecture, and machine learning.

Right now, I’m leading **Heterogeneous LLM Inference** at ML@Purdue and building computer-vision infrastructure with **Purdue ROV**.

Before returning to Purdue this semester, I worked as a software engineering intern with **Georgia Tech Research Institute’s Applied Systems Lab**. My work focused on performance testing, programming environments, and developer tooling: making it easier to build, test, and compare software across deployment configurations.
