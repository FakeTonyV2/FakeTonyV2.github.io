I'm Fiyin, a Computer Science undergraduate at Purdue University interested in systems, ML infrastructure, computer architecture, and the software that sits between algorithms and hardware.

I like building infrastructure that other software can build upon: runtimes, schedulers, developer tooling, data pipelines, and, increasingly, systems that make better use of heterogeneous compute.

Right now, I'm leading **Heterogeneous LLM Inference at ML@Purdue**, where we're investigating how CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs can cooperate during language-model inference. I'm also part of **Purdue ROV's Computer Vision team**, where I've worked on the infrastructure for running, testing, transporting, and observing computer-vision workloads on embedded hardware.

Before returning to Purdue this semester, I worked with **GTRI Applied Systems** as a software engineering/research intern, focusing largely on performance testing, programming environments, and developer tooling.

I'm pursuing Computer Science alongside a minor in **Microelectronics & Semiconductors**, with a focus on the microelectronics track. I expect to graduate in 2028 and am considering graduate study in computer engineering or computer science, particularly systems, architecture, and machine learning.

## What interests me

One idea has increasingly shaped the kinds of problems I enjoy:

**I like building software that makes other people more capable.**

Compilers, runtimes, schedulers, languages, infrastructure, and developer tools are interesting to me because their value compounds. A better application helps its users; a better platform can help people build entirely new applications.

That has pulled me toward systems performance, ML infrastructure, computer architecture, and heterogeneous computing. I'm especially interested in what happens as useful compute becomes cheaper and more abundant: what new systems become practical, how workloads should be scheduled across increasingly diverse hardware, and what abstractions we need to make all that compute actually usable.

I'm not particularly interested in restricting myself to one corner of computer science, though. If something presents a difficult systems problem, teaches me something new, or gives me an opportunity to build useful infrastructure, I'm probably interested.

Other recurring interests include investing, infrastructure, optimization, and generally learning how complicated systems work.
